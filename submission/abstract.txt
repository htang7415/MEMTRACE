Memory-enabled agents can carry information from untrusted retrieved content into future turns, but one-shot prompt-injection evaluations usually score only same-turn behavior. We introduce MEMTRACE, a trace-level benchmark and protocol that decomposes persistent-memory risk into retrieval exposure, poisoned-memory admission, trigger-time retrieval, unsafe proposal, policy-checker blocking, unsafe execution, and execution-format failure. In a 324-trace audited pilot with mlx-community/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-4bit and deterministic tools, one-shot attacks cause 7/24 unsafe executions for S0 and 6/24 for S1 and S2, while no delayed unsafe executions are observed across 72 stateful attacks per system. The permissive writer admits poisoned memory in 6/72 S1 episodes, and all six admitted memories are retrieved at the trigger, but none produces an unsafe proposal or executed violation; the provenance-aware reference writer admits none. An oracle-retrieved-memory calibration, excluded from main rates, yields 3/72 delayed executed violations, validating scorer sensitivity under a positive-control condition. MEMTRACE does not claim that persistent memory is safe; it shows that one-shot attack success, poisoned-memory admission, and delayed unsafe execution are distinct outcomes that require separate measurement.
